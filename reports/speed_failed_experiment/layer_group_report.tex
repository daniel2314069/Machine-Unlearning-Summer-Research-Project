\begin{frame}{Question}
  \begin{block}{Goal}
    Test whether erasing Snoopy with SPEED can be localized to coarse U-Net cross-attention value groups.
  \end{block}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  We edit only the original SPEED editable modules:
  \[
    \texttt{attn2.to\_v.weight}
  \]
  and compare three group-only variants against original SD and full SPEED.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  This is a group-level sufficiency test, not an exhaustive layer subset search.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Experimental Setup}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Target concept: Snoopy.
    \item Non-target concepts: Mickey, SpongeBob, Pikachu, Hello Kitty.
    \item General prior: first 1000 MS-COCO captions.
    \item Instance prompts: 80 templates, 10 images per template per concept.
    \item Each mode: 800 images per instance concept + 1000 COCO images.
    \item Metrics follow the SPEED Table 1-style evaluation:
      Snoopy CS, non-target FID, MSCOCO CS, MSCOCO FID.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Main Quantitative Result}
  \small
  \begin{table}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
      \toprule
      Mode & Snoopy CS $\downarrow$ & Avg non-target FID $\downarrow$ & MSCOCO CS $\uparrow$ & MSCOCO FID $\downarrow$ \\
      \midrule
      Original SD & 28.54 & 0.00 & 26.50 & 0.00 \\
      Full SPEED & \good{23.46} & 21.79 & 26.48 & 19.70 \\
      down only & 28.09 & 19.99 & \good{26.51} & 16.37 \\
      mid only & 28.74 & \good{14.36} & 26.47 & 13.39 \\
      up only & 26.34 & 14.66 & \good{26.52} & \good{11.46} \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
  \end{table}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  Lower Snoopy CS means stronger erasure.
  Lower FID and higher MSCOCO CS indicate better preservation.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What The Numbers Say}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Full SPEED gives the strongest Snoopy erasure:
      Snoopy CS drops from 28.54 to 23.46.
    \item No single coarse group reproduces full SPEED erasure.
    \item Up-only has partial erasure:
      Snoopy CS = 26.34, better than original but far from full SPEED.
    \item Mid-only and down-only are not sufficient for Snoopy erasure:
      their Snoopy CS remains close to original SD.
    \item Mid-only and up-only preserve non-target/general images better than full SPEED.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interpretation}
  \begin{block}{Primary takeaway}
    Snoopy erasure in SPEED is not localized to one coarse U-Net region.
    Full erasure appears to require coordinated updates across multiple cross-attention value groups.
  \end{block}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{block}{Secondary takeaway}
    Up-block value layers carry some erasure ability while preserving COCO better than full SPEED,
    suggesting a useful erasure--preservation tradeoff.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What This Is Not Claiming}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item This does not prove that every individual layer is necessary.
    \item This does not rank individual layers inside down/up/mid.
    \item This does not perform a $2^{16}$ layer subset search.
    \item This does not claim target CS alone is the objective; preservation metrics are part of the conclusion.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Abandoned Branch}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The earlier $C' = C_{0,\mathrm{refined}} \cup C_2$ experiment is not used as the main story here.
    \item Its behavior was harder to interpret and did not provide a clean layer-localization message.
    \item The layer-group result is cleaner: it directly answers where SPEED edits are sufficient or insufficient.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusion}
  \begin{block}{Reportable conclusion}
    Coarse group-only SPEED edits reveal a tradeoff:
    up-only and mid-only preserve better, but only full SPEED achieves strong Snoopy erasure.
  \end{block}

  \vspace{0.7em}
  This supports the view that SPEED's erasure effect is distributed across cross-attention value groups,
  not concentrated in a single U-Net block family.
\end{frame}
